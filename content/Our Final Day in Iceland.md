We have come to our final day at sea on the Viking Mars.  We've spent it anchored in the beautiful cove pictured above, in the Westman Islands.  The weather has turned from rainy and cold to perfect: warm sunshine and temperatures in the 50s.  Our current location is indescribably beautiful. There are thousands of bird flying around, including puffins nesting on the grassy upper parts of the mountain; there are sheep grazing on the very steep hillsides, and we are floating here taking it all in.  What a treat.  As I write this (overlooking the scene above) we have just about circumnavigated the island of Iceland.  Tonight we will sail along the southern coast of Iceland and end our journey where we started: Rekyjavik.  

We've seen many wonderful things in the past week. The mountains, the lava fields, the glaciers, the rivers and waterfalls.  The sheep and puffins and Icelandic horses.  I would encourage everyone to go see Iceland. It is a totally unique place.  The very picturesque village houses with their corrugated steel sidings and functional Icelandic architecture.  The natural beauty here rivals anywhere I've ever travelled.  And seeing Iceland by ship is certainly a great option.  Car travel is a bit difficult here due to all of the fjords, glaciers and volcanic natural obstacles.  It can take a few hours to go from a place that is just a few miles away as a the crow flies.  No matter how you decide to see Iceland, I would really recommend going.

Some people may remember that in 1973 there was a  huge volcanic eruption on this island that destroyed many homes and buildings.  We were able to take a walk in the area known as Hraunskógur (the Lava Woods).  In this area, volunteers each year plant new trees in the lava fields covering the area destroyed by the 1973 earthquake. As a result of this effort (since the year 2000) the lava is starting to be covered by trees and shrubs. Have a look:
