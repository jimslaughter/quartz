Yesterday we took a day-long tour to visit  *Dettifoss*, which is said to be the most powerful waterfall in Europe.  On the way there we were able to see some other sights as well.  The weather was beautiful again.  We've been really lucky with the weather so far.    

The tour started from Akureyri, where our ship was docked.  Akureyri is the largest city in the north of Iceland. It even has its own international airport. It sits at the end of a very long fjord. After we marveled at Dettifoss for a while, we visited a lake called Lake Myvatn (we were told the name translates to "Midge Lake") to see some small volcanic craters. To be honest, the craters weren't that interesting, and there were midges (tiny flying insects) everywhere. The lake is nutrient rich, famous for birdlife and home to more species of ducks than almost anywhere else in the world.  There was a small group of Icelandic horses munching on grass and whisking midges off with their tails.  So that was fun to see. There are lots of Icelandic horses in this part of the country and they have quicky become my favorite breed of horse.  

![[Pasted image 20260807000630.png]]

Leaving the midges behind, our bus whisked us back to the ship just in time for departure for our next port of call, Seyðisfjörður.  We had dinner in our usual spot.  The seas were quite a bit rougher than anything we'd experienced on this voyage, but the ship's stablilizer system was working overtime to keep things more or less on an even keel.  

So ended another great day on our trip.  While in Seyðisfjörður we plan to go on a puffin tour, so we will hope that the puffins cooperate and make an appearance.
